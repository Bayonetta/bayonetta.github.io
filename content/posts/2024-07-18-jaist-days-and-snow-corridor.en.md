In April 2024 I arrived at JAIST in Nomi, Ishikawa. The weather was the hardest thing to get used to. Spring comes slowly in Hokuriku, and the wind still carries winter's dampness.

## Weather and campus rhythm

The Sea of Japan coast has many cloudy winter days, with heavy, wet snow. The seasons feel more sharply separated than in a city. The campus is away from the center, so people disappear quickly after class and the laboratory lights remain. There are few interruptions in a day.

## Reading seminars and whiteboards

I joined Professor Ryuhei Uehara's laboratory, whose field is the computational complexity of combinatorial puzzles. I was still deciding what problem to work on.

## From engineer to graduate student

The difference is who defines the problem. Engineering starts with a given requirement; in research, you first have to explain what “solved” means, and the answer may be negative. During the first months I kept looking for the feeling of a release, but it never came. There are no version numbers here, only an unfinished argument.

## From Amaharashi Coast to the Snow Corridor

In May, I spent several days with new friends on the Tateyama Kurobe Alpine Route to see the Snow Corridor. The cleared passage is usually open from April to June, subject to that year's official schedule. Altitude turns the seasons back: spring had already arrived below, while tall snow walls remained on both sides.

## How the environment changed my life

Hokuriku is quiet and sparsely populated, which makes it a good place to do research. I do not know how others feel, but I became comfortable here.

Before enrolling, I often dreamed about school. The scenes were vague, but they were always from my student years. After enrolling, those dreams stopped. It felt as if the wish had finally come true, and I was satisfied.

The only inconvenience is the distance to the supermarket and the infrequent buses. Going out requires planning because a round trip often takes two or three hours. Once I got used to it, it was not a serious problem.
