I had planned to travel to Taiwan over the May Day holiday with a colleague. Because of an issue with his household registration, his Taiwan travel permit never came through. My paperwork and flights were already arranged, so I set off on my own.

## Day 1: Shenzhen / Hong Kong / Taipei

Flights directly from Hong Kong were cheaper, so I flew from Beijing to Shenzhen and met an old local friend for a meal. In the afternoon, I took a boat from Shekou Pier straight to Hong Kong International Airport on the island. The airport was enormous, and the service from Hong Kong staff was awful: even while I was washing my hands, a cleaner yelled at me. I couldn't understand the Cantonese anyway.

I shook off that mood and soon boarded the plane. An hour later, I landed at Taoyuan Airport and my Taiwan trip began. From there I took a service that really was called a rickshaw, despite being offered by some travel company on Ctrip. It was surprisingly efficient. In Taipei, I stayed at a hostel near Ximending, which was very conveniently located. It was so hot. I had heard typhoon season would arrive in the following months, so I rushed to visit in May.

## Day 3: Taipei / Taichung / Sun Moon Lake

The next day I went to Eslite Bookstore and bought *The Great River* and an English edition of Greek mythology. (A note added later: even ten years on, I still haven't read either one.) I had beef noodles for lunch, though they didn't look especially authentic.

In the afternoon, I took the high-speed rail to Taichung. I wasn't planning to stay there, so I transferred straight to a bus for Sun Moon Lake. I heard most people stayed on the Shuishe side, but I like doing my own thing, so I booked a comfortable room near Ita Thao Pier instead. There was even a 7-Eleven on that little island. Very convenient.

By the way, Nantou is Taiwan's only landlocked county. Such an unusual bit of geography.

## Days 5–6: Tainan / Kenting

On Day 5, I explored Tainan, visiting the Anping Tree House and several other historic sites. This seemed to be one of the earliest places in Taiwan where civilization took root, so there were plenty of historic spots.

On the afternoon of Day 6, I set off for Kenting. That night, I ate my fill at the night market right outside. The fruit called “custard apple” made me laugh, though it wasn't as sweet as the one I'd had in Hong Kong.

## Day 6: Kenting

Since I didn't have a driver's license, I left my travel permit with the rental shop owner as a deposit and rented an electric scooter. Riding around on a little scooter in flip-flops felt wonderfully carefree. Taiwan's convenience stores had so many tasty drinks; even after several days, I still hadn't tried them all.

## Day 7: Kaohsiung

The next day I headed straight to Kaohsiung and stayed high up in the 85 Sky Tower. Because of my anxiety, I spent the whole night on edge. Still, having that huge room all to myself felt great. The city itself seemed rather run-down to me, even more so than Taipei.

## Day 8: Return

I had a connecting ticket from Kaohsiung to Nanjing, then flew from Nanjing back to my little home in Beijing that evening.

I'd like to return someday and explore the other half of Taiwan, the east coast. But with Tsai Ing-wen about to take office, I felt cross-strait relations might lose their peaceful footing. I wondered when traveling between the two sides would feel easy again.
