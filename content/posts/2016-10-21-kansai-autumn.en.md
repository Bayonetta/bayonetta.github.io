In autumn 2016, the whole team traveled to Kansai and spent several days between Kyoto and Osaka. What I remember most is the signage. I had not seriously studied Japanese, but kanji let me guess many shop signs and station names. It created the illusion that I could understand the country.

## The illusion of understanding

The kanji on Kyoto bus stops and Osaka shopping streets made reading surprisingly easy. Words such as exit, information, and no smoking needed almost no translation. I could read station names, but not the fast syllables in announcements; I could understand a sign, but not the greeting from a shop assistant.

We stayed at the Crown Hotel in Enoshima, with a Keihan station downstairs. After landing, we bought a two-day Osaka Amazing Pass and ICOCA cards.

## Days 1–2: Osaka

We walked almost 30,000 steps a day to visit as many pass attractions as possible, from the aquarium to Dotonbori.

## Days 3–4: Kyoto

Not understanding the train categories, I chose the trains with seats and unknowingly selected the slowest ones. From Keihan Shijo to Nijo Castle, we walked almost nonstop again.

## Day 5: Osaka

We filled in the missed attractions and watched *Your Name* at a Umeda cinema. I understood almost nothing and followed online Chinese subtitles for the whole film. It was a memorable experience. I also bought an art collection by Non Oda in Nipponbashi and quietly took it home.

## Day 6: Home

After returning, I felt that traveling in Japan was wonderful. I wanted to go again—or perhaps return with a different identity.
