During the first week of 2019, I traveled alone through Kyushu using a Seishun 18 Ticket: Kyoto to Hiroshima, then Fukuoka, and finally from Kumamoto to Beppu in Oita. I had been in Japan for two months and still did not know what would come after graduation. I chose a hot-spring town for New Year's because I wanted this otherwise unmarked point in time to have a visible boundary.

## Changing phases

That night I thought mostly about transitions themselves. Leaving a job, becoming a student, moving from China to Japan—none of these identities changed on one particular day. Daily life changed first, and only later did I notice it in a quiet moment. New Year's simply made that delay more visible: a calendar page turns, but a person does not automatically enter a new phase.

I did not make resolutions. I only reordered the things I was already doing by whether they could continue: language study, exam preparation, and the next destination.

## The time scale of long-term goals

Spending New Year's alone also made the time scale clearer. The path after graduating in spring 2020 and the possibility of further study were still uncertain. All I could do was choose actions that would remain useful over a long period, rather than depend on how I felt on one particular day.

I hope everything goes well. Perhaps this will be the easiest part of my future life.
