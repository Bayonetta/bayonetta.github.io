In early January, the Noto Peninsula earthquake occurred while the Hokuriku exam was being held online overseas, so I was not affected. After half a month of waiting, I received the acceptance notice on January 19. I was overjoyed; after four years, the result had finally arrived.

In early February, I returned to Kyoto once again for what I hoped would be my final graduate-school entrance exam: Kyoto University, one of Japan's top universities and a long-held dream. It was my third and final attempt.

I passed the written exam and reached the interview stage. I was seventh in the written ranking, but only six people would be admitted. There was danger, but also hope.

After another half month of waiting, I learned that the candidates ahead of me were excellent. The ranking did not change after the interviews, and I narrowly missed admission in seventh place.

I gathered myself and prepared for a new master's life. After four years of stillness, the gears of my life had begun to turn again.
