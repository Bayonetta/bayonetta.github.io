Near graduation, three friends and I started our graduation trip.

## Days 1–3: Chengdu

Our long-awaited flight took us from Wuhan to Chengdu at night. The heat and humidity felt just like Wuhan, only stronger. At the guesthouse I discovered that I had bought the return flight in the wrong direction. A frantic correction fixed that life bug.

We spent two days walking through Kuanzhai Alley, Wuhou Shrine, and Du Fu Thatched Cottage, surrounded by summer greenery and traditional courtyards. At night we tried Sichuan hot pot. The clear broth slowly turned red, so I ended up experiencing the real spicy version. Even the sesame-oil dipping sauce was new to me, but I decided to follow local customs.

## Day 4: Dujiangyan

We took an early high-speed train to Dujiangyan and gave up Qingcheng Mountain for lack of time. The scale of the irrigation project was impressive even before entering: a classic example of people reshaping nature. We walked uphill and downhill all day and ate plenty of cold rice cakes. I was reluctantly persuaded to buy yak jerky, then discovered it was delicious and quickly shared it with my friends and family.

## Days 5–6: Jiuzhaigou

The eight-hour bus ride was the longest of my life, and the roadside toilets charged money. On the way I learned that my thesis and program had to be burned to a disc, so I used mobile data to send the graduation project to a friend for help. Mountain-road phone reception was terrifying.

At Jiuzhaigou the mountain air was cold enough for a quilt in June. We queued at the entrance in a freezing wind, so I bought a blue jacket at an unreasonable price. It was wonderfully warm. Inside, the green-blue water looked unreal. Eight hours on the bus had been worth it.

## Day 7: Huanglong

We shared a car for the day and met a kind Japanese woman named Yuko, who spoke excellent Chinese. She had grown up in the United States, worked at Sony, and was studying Chinese in Beijing. I had once considered studying in Japan, but at that time decided to graduate and find a job at home.

Huanglong was more than 3,000 meters above sea level and the views were spectacular. After several days I felt cleansed and unusually relaxed.

## Day 8: Home

Another eight-hour bus ride brought us back to Chengdu. At the bus terminal I saw a map full of Sichuan attractions and decided there would be plenty of time to visit them one by one. After flying home, a friend's parents hosted us for a night and cooked a wonderful meal. We laughed at a video comment saying “press Ctrl+W to close the comments”. The next day we returned to school to graduate for real. I was reluctant to leave.
